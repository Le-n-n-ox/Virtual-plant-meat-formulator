## Which records can train which estimate

- The current candidate for a composition-only HME hardness/chewiness baseline is the **42 HME rows** in the 2023 FoodML table. They cover six proximate-composition inputs plus target moisture, across two source studies and eight HME source groups. Thirty rows have a supplemental Ferawati process crosswalk; 12 Chiang soy/WG rows do not. FoodML has no extrusion-batch or lot IDs. Evaluation must hold out whole studies and groups; random row splits would overstate transfer. The paper's published errors use an additional 12 mechanical-elongation samples and are not a performance estimate for this HME subset.
- A separate process-aware candidate is the **30 FoodML/Ferawati rows**, enriched in place from Tables A3/A4 with target moisture, screw speed, barrel zones, mass flow, SME, and source-reported texture summaries. This is still one source study and four ingredient groups, with no batch IDs. The sample-number-to-table-ID mapping and 0.0098 N per table-reported g factor are inferred from row alignment; A3/A4 disagree on the final FBI-local target moisture (68% vs 70%). Report those caveats and do not double-count them as 30 extra samples.
- Kantanen (2022) supplies **17 faba protein isolate/concentrate HME summaries** across 15 distinct settings, with measured hardness, chewiness, process conditions, and defined units. It is a separate, small process-response dataset; its chewiness in joules and its inputs should not be silently merged into the FoodML feature table.
- Knaapila (2024) supplies **six whole-faba analog formulations** with process and composition metadata. The source omits mechanical measurement units, and its WAC table scale does not match the stated method scale, so these values support analog lookup and coverage display only.
- Jeon (2023) supplies **six full-fat-soy HME formulations** at one fixed process setting. Its reported targets have units but no hardness, and the article does not report independent extrusion-batch replication or the formulation percentage basis. Keep it as condition-specific comparative evidence.
- Mektun & Kijroongrojana (2026) supplies **nine HME temperature/moisture conditions** for one fixed 70:30 mung-bean protein-isolate:wheat-gluten blend, including hardness and chewiness. It can support a source-specific response surface for that precise formulation and extruder only; its design is too small to generalize to Kenyan whole foods. The center-condition triplicate and n=10 instrument measurements are separate replication levels. Table 5 equations stay disabled until verified against the PDF.
- Bulgaru et al. (2025) supplies **eight source summaries** at two temperature profiles for pea-isolate controls and blends with soryz, chickpea, or hazelnut meal. It adds crop comparisons, including sorghum and chickpea flour, but has only two profiles per blend, unspecified extrusion-batch replication, and non-Kenyan ingredient varieties.
- KU Leuven's 115 soy texture readings lack specimen identifiers for pairing separate metrics; preserve the metric rows as source observations. The 56 CSIRO runs and 299 texture pieces are **low-moisture** data and remain out of HME training.

## No-lab prediction route

The data support developing a literature-trained research estimator without project-owned lab measurements; a fitted estimator has not yet been implemented in this project. Keep the two routes separate: use the 42 FoodML HME rows for a composition-only baseline, and use only records with verified process conditions for process-aware screening. Never mix low-moisture extrusion or mechanical elongation into HME targets. Compare performance by leaving out complete source groups; do not reuse the 2023 paper's published scores as this project's validation result. The 2023 paper's reported metrics came from its full mixed-method dataset and do not establish Kenyan accuracy.

The available data are small, uneven, and dominated by isolates/concentrates. Research literature does not demonstrate that texture predictions transfer accurately to Kenyan formulations when there are no Kenyan target labels. The estimator should therefore be an evidence-bounded screening aid: show the nearest supporting source groups, target units, and measured ranges; return a nearest-literature comparison or “insufficient supporting evidence” outside those groups. Do not present an out-of-domain numeric texture value as a supported prediction. A language model can explain the evidence but must not generate texture targets.

The 2024 faba study modestly broadens ingredient-form coverage, but it does not establish performance for Kenyan beans, cereals, or their blends. Nutrition composition from KFCT can supply ingredient features only where identities and preparation states match; nutrient data alone cannot determine water-holding capacity, protein functionality, fibrous structure, or extrusion response.

## Required data handling

- Keep `processing.regime` explicit: HME, low-moisture extrusion, and mechanical elongation are separate targets.
- Preserve source-study, formulation/group, and run identifiers. Do not treat test cuts or repeated measurements as independent extrusion runs.
- Retain means, standard deviations, replicate counts, measurement methods, directions, units, and source rows where the article reports them.
- Preserve both ingredient form and composition: whole flour/groat, concentrate, or isolate are not interchangeable categories.
- Fit/evaluate only from a record set with sufficient input and target fields for that specific model; never silently impute missing processing or ingredient properties.
- Check applicability before prediction. Out-of-domain inputs should produce a nearest-source summary or no numeric result.
- Label current outputs as literature-derived estimates, not validated or locally calibrated results.

## Data boundary

No lab is required to produce a first research estimator. A lab or independent HME measurement partner would be required only to claim local experimental validation or to quantify accuracy for Kenyan formulations. Without that validation, the system can still support evidence-bounded screening, literature comparisons, and nutrition-constrained formulation exploration, while being explicit about uncertainty and unsupported ingredients.
