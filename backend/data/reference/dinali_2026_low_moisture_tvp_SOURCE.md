# Dinali et al. (2026) low-moisture flour TVP transcription

**Article:** Malsha Dinali, Isuru Wijesekara, Rumesh Liyanage, Lisa Newman, Benu Adhikari, Mayumi Silva, and Jayani Chandrapala. “Structural, and functional characterisation of texturized mung bean, cowpea, soy flours, and soy protein isolates for meat analogue applications.” *Food Structure* 48 (2026), 100528. DOI: [10.1016/j.foostr.2026.100528](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foostr.2026.100528).

The table transcription in `dinali_2026_low_moisture_flour_tvp_table1_table3.csv` contains the mung-bean-flour (T-MBF) and cowpea-flour (T-CPF) rows from Tables 1 and 3. The published article is open access under CC BY 4.0; the transcription is attributed to the article, and its units and study-specific conditions are preserved. [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

These are **low-moisture extrusion** outcomes, not HME. Flour raw materials came from seeds purchased in Sri Lanka, then washed, soaked in distilled water at 1:3 w/v for 6 hours at 28 ± 1 °C, drained, oven-dried at 55 °C for 16 hours, milled, and sieved through 1 mm. Flour extrusion used barrel zones at 60/80/100/110/115 °C, 46 g/min solids, 20 mL/min water, 200 rpm, a 115 °C die, and calculated feed moisture of about 35.5–36.5%. Extrudates were dried below 10% moisture. Table 1 reports the resulting dry texturized products; Table 3 reports texture after 90-minute rehydration and 30-minute draining. Both are means ± SD from three replicates. The article does not state a unit for chewiness in Table 3; that field is deliberately blank.

The two flour records are useful for a separate low-moisture research comparison involving mung-bean flour and cowpea flour. They are not Kenyan ingredient-lot measurements, do not cover the project's HME process, and must not be pooled with HME training targets. They provide no Kenyan validation. No raw replicate files were provided with the article; only the published table summaries were transcribed.
